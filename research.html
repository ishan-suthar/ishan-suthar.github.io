Sequential target trials for acute decompensated heart failure

University of South Carolina, Aug 2023 to now

Can we rigorously emulate a randomized trial from hospital records for acutely ill patients, when the sickness that drives treatment also drives outcomes? My dissertation compares treatment strategies for acute decompensated heart failure by specifying the trial first, then emulating it from observational data.

  • Specified eligibility, treatment strategies, follow-up rules and outcomes before any analysis
  • Built SQL extraction and derivation pipelines from raw relational tables, version-controlled so every analysis dataset can be rebuilt and traced to source
  • Used inverse probability weighting for time-varying confounding and cloning with grace periods to remove immortal time bias
  • Designed as a clinical decision module: estimates for the decision points clinicians actually face, not just population averages

This work is the foundation for TriEmulate. Full methods and findings are being prepared for publication.

National Medicaid reimbursement database

Part of the NIH periodontal study

Cost analysis needs procedure-level prices, but published state fee schedules are inconsistent and often incomplete. I built a national, population-weighted reimbursement database across state fee schedules and dental procedure codes.

  • Developed a code-level ratio-imputation method to recover values that states systematically leave unpublished
  • Weighted state figures by population so national estimates reflect where patients actually are
  • Supports procedure-level cost analysis for payer coverage decisions
