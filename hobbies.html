Beyond work

The things that keep me competitive, patient and grounded. Most of them turn out to use the same habits of mind as research.

Badminton

Player since high school, and coach

Badminton has been the one constant through medical school, my MPH and my PhD. It followed me from India to the US, and every new city starts with finding a new court.

Baking

Bread, mostly, and espresso

Baking is the closest thing to bench science I do at home. Wild yeast, hydration, fermentation time and temperature are variables to control, and every loaf is a small experiment. It asks for the same patience as long-term cohort research.

Long-fermented sourdough is the anchor, but I also bake focaccia, pizza on a poolish, bagels, baguettes, bao and stuffed burger buns. The fast, forgiving bakes balance out the slow ones. Dialing in espresso at home is the same problem on a much shorter timescale.

Markets

Investing and trading research

I approach markets the way I approach epidemiology. Before any position I look at earnings, macro conditions, sector rotation and price structure, because speculation without a thesis is just noise.

I build my own indicators and systematic strategies in Pine Script on TradingView. Working with real-world health data has made me careful about signal versus noise, confounding and survivorship bias, and about the limits of any model. No strategy is infallible, and risk management matters most. For the research version of this, see LiquidAI.
